# Funding and free resources

Everything below is either free or free to a registered nonprofit. It is written
for the Mill Creek-AR Learning Center specifically: a small computer-education
centre in Accra teaching children aged 8–14, working with the AD2 Alumni Foundation.

**Verify before you rely on anything here.** Eligibility rules, amounts and
deadlines move faster than a document does. Treat each entry as a lead with a
link, not a promise.

---

## 1. Settle eligibility first — it gates almost everything

Nearly every programme below asks one question: *are you a registered nonprofit,
and where?* There are two routes, and the answer decides which doors open.

| Route | Opens | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Through the AD2 Alumni Foundation** (if it holds US 501(c)(3) status) | US corporate giving, TechSoup US, Google for Nonprofits via the US entity, US donor tax receipts, many global foundations | The foundation must be willing to be the applicant and the fiscal sponsor, and to account for funds spent in Ghana |
| **A Ghana-registered entity** (company limited by guarantee, registered with the Registrar-General and the Department of Social Welfare) | MTN Ghana Foundation, Vodafone/Telecel Ghana Foundation, Ghanaian government programmes, local corporate CSR, most Africa-focused funders | Registration takes time and ongoing filing; budget for it |

**Do both if you can.** A Ghanaian entity partnered with a US foundation is the
strongest position: local legitimacy plus access to US philanthropy.

Two related jobs that block funders' due diligence if left undone:

- Register as a **data controller** with Ghana's Data Protection Commission.
  You process children's data, which the Data Protection Act 2012 treats as
  special personal data. Serious funders check.
- Have a written **child safeguarding policy**. Many funders will not release
  money to an organisation working with children without one, and they will ask
  to see it rather than take your word.

## 4. Hardware

Refurbished-computer charities ship to African schools. Most ask you to cover
shipping and customs, which is a real cost — budget for it and ask each one
what a container or pallet to Accra actually lands at.

- **[Computer Aid International](https://www.computeraid.org/)** (UK) — over
  267,000 refurbished computers to education and nonprofits in 110+ countries.
- **[World Computer Exchange](https://worldcomputerexchange.org/)** (US) — the
  longest-running US nonprofit in this space.
- **IT Schools Africa** and **[Computers2Africa](https://computers2africa.org/)**
  (UK) — Africa-focused, tens of thousands of machines delivered.
- **Raspberry Pi** — for the Kolibri server and as cheap workstations. A single
  Pi serving Kolibri to a classroom over local Wi-Fi is the best value in this
  whole document.

Two things to specify when you ask: **Linux-capable machines** (no Windows
licence cost, and they run better on old hardware), and a **UPS or battery
backup** for the server, because a power cut mid-write can corrupt it.

## 5. Grant funders

### Ghana and West Africa
- **MTN Ghana Foundation** — digital literacy and skills training for youth is an
  explicit focus area; also runs STEM scholarships prioritising ICT, and support
  for youth-, women- and PwD-led enterprises. Local, relevant, and reachable.
- **Telecel/Vodafone Ghana Foundation** — long-running education and digital
  inclusion programmes.
- **Ghana Tech Lab, NEIP, and the Ministry of Communication and Digitalisation** —
  government-linked digital skills programmes; partnership is often easier to
  win than cash.
- **Local bank and mining company CSR** — under-used by small organisations, and
  decisions are made in Accra by people you can actually meet.

### Africa-wide
- **[Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/what-we-do/focus-areas/digital/)** —
  Young Africa Works, with a large digital-skills commitment across Africa and an
  active Ghana digital-economy portfolio. Targets young people 15–35 and aims for
  70% young women. Your learners are younger than their core target, so pitch the
  **pipeline** argument: you are building the 8–14 foundation their 15–35
  programmes later depend on.
- **Jacobs Foundation**, **Global Fund for Children**, **Segal Family Foundation** —
  child- and youth-focused, and used to funding small organisations.
- **UNICEF Innovation Fund**, **Google.org** — larger, more competitive, longer cycles.

### Crowdfunding
- **[GlobalGiving Accelerator](https://www.globalgiving.org/accelerator/)** — open to
  any registered nonprofit worldwide. Complete the training, raise at least
  **$5,000 from 40+ distinct donors**, and you earn a permanent listing on the
  GlobalGiving platform. Realistic, and the permanent listing keeps paying off.
- **Zeffy** — already wired into this site, and it takes **0% platform fee**, which
  is unusually good. Keep it as the primary channel.

## 6. The argument that wins applications

### Ghana's own curriculum just moved your way

NaCCA's revised basic-school curriculum introduces **coding, computational
thinking, and foundational AI literacy from kindergarten through JHS**, with
computational thinking written into both the Computing and Mathematics
syllabuses. Schools now have a mandate they are not yet resourced to deliver.

That is your case, and it is a strong one:

> The curriculum now requires computational thinking from KG through JHS. Most
> basic schools in our catchment have neither the equipment nor a teacher trained
> to deliver it. We already do, for children aged 8–14, and we can show exactly
> what each learner completed.

Map each module to its curriculum reference — the `modules.ges_curriculum_ref`
column exists for precisely this — and put the mapping in every application.
It converts you from "an after-school activity" into "delivering the national
curriculum where the state cannot yet reach."

### Show numbers, not adjectives

Funders fund what is measured. Now that the academy is running, you can report
real figures instead of estimates:

- learners enrolled, and how many are girls
- assignments completed and reviewed, per term
- student web pages published
- attendance and retention across a term
- progression: how many moved from blocks to typed code

`teach/report.php` produces these for a date range. Export it into every report
and application. A centre that can say *"47 learners, 61% girls, 312 assignments
completed, 23 pages published this term"* is in a different category from one
that says it is making a difference.

### Let the children's own work do the talking

The strongest artefact you have is a child's published web page. It is
concrete, it is theirs, it demonstrates the outcome directly — and because pages
carry no identifying information and are staff-reviewed before publication, it
is safe to share with a funder in a way a photograph of a child is not.

## 7. A realistic first ninety days

**Weeks 1–4 — the paperwork that unlocks everything else**
Settle the legal entity question. Register with the Data Protection Commission.
Write the safeguarding policy and the guardian consent form, in English and Twi.
Get nonprofit validation through Goodstack.

**Weeks 5–8 — claim the free tier**
Google for Nonprofits and the Ad Grant. TechSoup. Register a Code Club. Set up a
Cloudflare account. Install Kolibri on a Pi. Download Blockly Games into `lab/`.
None of this costs money; all of it costs someone's attention.

**Weeks 9–12 — the first asks**
Apply to the GlobalGiving Accelerator. Write to Computer Aid International and
World Computer Exchange about hardware, and ask about shipping to Accra. Approach
MTN Ghana Foundation with the curriculum-gap argument and one term of real
numbers from `teach/report.php`.

**Then, and only then**, go after the large multi-year funders. They want to see
a term of delivery and real data. By that point you will have both.

## 8. What not to do

- **Do not put children's faces or names in a funding application.** Use their
  work. It is more persuasive and it carries no safeguarding risk.
- **Do not accept restricted funding you cannot deliver.** A grant with reporting
  obligations you miss costs more than it pays, and it closes that door.
- **Do not build the programme around one funder.** Three small grants and a
  sponsor base beat one large grant that ends.
- **Do not let free software trap you.** Everything recommended here is open
  source or has an export path. Check that before adopting anything new.
